Core strength

Portfolio risk modeling

Risk comes before return in everything I build. When I evaluate a strategy or a portfolio, I follow a seven-step process, from choosing the metrics through to ongoing monitoring. At Quantecko I use it to make sure every investor-facing number holds up under stress.

σStandard deviationTotal volatility of returns
VaRValue-at-RiskLoss threshold at a given confidence
CVaRConditional VaRAverage loss beyond the VaR tail
βBetaSensitivity to the market
MDDMax drawdownWorst peak-to-trough decline

  1. Define risk metrics

    Choose measures that match the mandate and the investor's risk tolerance: volatility, VaR, CVaR, beta and maximum drawdown.

  2. Collect data

    Gather historical prices, returns, volatility and other risk factors over a period long enough to cover different market conditions.

    In practiceQuantecko's backtesting simulator runs on 7+ years of data across volatility regimes, including the 2020 crash.

  3. Clean and prepare

    Handle missing values and outliers, correct biases and inconsistencies, then turn prices into returns and standardize them.

  4. Model the risk

    Pick the technique that suits the strategy and how its returns behave.

    Variance-covarianceFast and analytical. Assumes normally distributed returns.
    Historical simulationReplays real history, so fat tails and non-normal returns are captured.
    Monte CarloThousands of simulated paths, with flexible distributions and correlations.
    Factor modelsCAPM, Fama-French 3-factor and Carhart 4-factor for systematic risk.

  5. Analyze exposures

    Read the model output for factor exposures, concentration risk and sensitivity to market moves.

  6. Manage the risk

    Bring the portfolio back within tolerance through diversification, hedging with derivatives, position sizing or a change in allocation.

    In practiceAt Datamatics I tracked live strategies, attributed P&L and flagged alpha decay early, so risk was caught before it reached the book.

  7. Monitor and reassess

    Track exposure and performance continuously, and update the model as markets and holdings change.

    In practiceQuantecko's live track record is fully broker-verified, and that held up through a UAE allocator's final due diligence.

Core skill

Option pricing models

To find mispriced options, you need a fair value you can trust. I price the same contract with four independent models and compare each one with the market price. When the models agree with each other but disagree with the market, the gap is worth investigating. Quantecko runs a systematic options strategy, so this is everyday work.

Black-Scholes-Merton

Closed form

Values a European option as the discounted expected payoff under risk-neutral pricing, with the risk-free rate as the discount factor. The underlying follows geometric Brownian motion, and the Black-Scholes PDE has an exact solution.

C = S·N(d₁) − K·e−rT·N(d₂)

Inputs: spot, strike, rate, time to expiry, volatility

Monte Carlo simulation

Simulation

Samples many random future paths of the underlying, computes the payoff on each one, and averages the discounted results. It works for path-dependent and exotic payoffs where closed forms don't exist.

V ≈ e−rT · (1/n) Σ payoff(ST(i))

Antithetic variates reduce the variance

Heston model

Stochastic volatility

Lets volatility move on its own mean-reverting process, correlated with the price. This captures the volatility smile and skew that a single constant σ misses, which matters most for volatile underlyings.

dv = κ(θ − v)dt + ξ√v dWv

Parameters: v₀, κ, θ, ξ, ρ

Finite difference methods

Numerical PDE

Discretizes price and time into a grid and solves the pricing PDE step by step, backwards from expiry. It handles contracts with no closed-form solution, such as American or barrier options.

∂V/∂t + ½σ²S²·∂²V/∂S² + rS·∂V/∂S − rV = 0

Crank–Nicolson scheme with a tridiagonal solver
